Open source · AI powered

An open-source, AI-powered, hypothetical administration · 2028–2032

What if we chose a team for what they've done, and judged its success by how much it helps the people who've had the fewest chances?

This isn't one person's list or any party's. It's an open-source, AI-powered democracy experiment that belongs to everyone. AI made the first picks. Now you can vote, suggest someone else, and tell us why. Your feedback goes back through AI to help shape the next version. Every pick, rule and line of code is public on GitHub.

No party test. No quotas. Just the strongest track record for each job. For this hypothetical ticket, McConaughey starts as a strong lead for president. Vote for any pick in every seat, or write in someone you think is better and tell us why.

AI reviews the votes, write-ins and feedback periodically and updates the team.

Why this exists

"My hope here is that we can find a common ground, break through any obstacles that may have kept us apart."Palmer Joss (Matthew McConaughey), Contact, 1997

America has seen independent and third-party runs before. Ross Perot won almost 19% of the vote in 1992. Ralph Nader ran in 2000. Neither gave voters a lasting third choice. Today, both major parties face pressure from their edges, while many people are looking for a way to meet in the middle.

This is one idea for that middle road: not left against right, but one country working together. Start with people who have done the work, then let everyone vote, suggest changes, and add names. Balance is not a quota. It's what we're working toward.

The fine print

This is an independent citizen project. Nobody named here has endorsed it, agreed to a role, or been asked yet. Matthew McConaughey's October 2026 video urged people to consider independent candidates. It endorsed no one and wasn't about him running. This page is a hypothetical built on that idea.

How the picks were made. Imagine picking a team for one shared mission: using AI where it helps, making power cheap and plentiful, making healthcare and education easier to reach, opening doors for people left behind, and keeping government clean and open. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini each chose someone for every seat independently, without seeing the others' answers. They scored six things: speed, skill with AI, results for people with the fewest opportunities, a clean record, readiness to serve through 2028–2032, and trust across differences. Party, gender and race were not scored. When they agreed, that became the pick. When they split, the strongest evidence decided. "Close call" means exactly that. Every nominee would publicly disclose their holdings and step away from decisions affecting their business or a funder's. Disagree with a choice? Vote, write in a name, or propose a change through a GitHub pull request.

How voting works. One vote per seat per browser. Change it anytime, or click your choice again to take it back. Voting starts with a Cloudflare human check (one check covers 30 minutes of voting), and each network has a daily cap. We don't store IP addresses, only a hash that changes every day. It's not bot-proof. Nothing is. If we see gaming, we'll tighten it and say so.

Write-ins. Anyone can write in their own pick for any seat. An AI gives each new write-in a quick sniff test (a real person, a real reason, no abuse) without judging their politics. Once three different people write in the same name, it joins that seat's list and anyone can back it with one tap. Once a seat has 10 or more votes, its list sorts by votes, so whoever people keep writing in rises to the top, past the AI picks if that's what voters want. Reasons that pass the sniff test show on the seat.
